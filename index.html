Nubia

Five thousand years of life along the Nile in northern Sudan: its kingdoms, languages, songs and painted homes.

A river and its kingdoms

Nubia follows the Nile from Aswan in Egypt south into Sudan. Some of Africa’s oldest states grew here.

Kerma, about 2500 BCE
The first large city-state of the upper Nile.
Kingdom of Kush, about 1000 BCE – 350 CE
Capitals at Napata and Meroë. The pyramids of Meroë still stand.
Christian kingdoms, 6th – 15th century
Nobatia, Makuria and Alodia, with Old Nubian written in its own script.
1960s, Wadi Halfa
The High Dam flooded Nubian villages. Thousands of families were resettled far from the river.

Languages

Nubian languages belong to the Nilo-Saharan family. In Sudan, Nobiin and Dongolawi are the best known. Both are spoken at home and in song, and both are under pressure from Arabic in schools and cities.

Music and craft

  • Songs sung to the tambour, the lyre of the Nile valley, and to drums at weddings.
  • House walls painted with geometric patterns in white, ochre, blue and red.
  • Palm-fibre baskets, mats and leatherwork.
